Crispy Korean
Fried Chicken

Double-fried for a shattering crust, glazed in a fiery gochujang sauce. Choose your cut — tenders or traditional bone-in wings.

✦
Prep 20 min
Marinade 1+ hr
Cook 25 min
Serves 4
Servings
4
Style
Ingredients
Gochujang Glaze
  • 192ggochujang
  • 90mlKorean rice syrup or honey
  • 80mldark soy sauce or regular
  • 90mlwhite distilled vinegar
  • 80mlsweet soy sauce
  • 120gketchup
  • 7garlic cloves, finely minced
Chicken — Tenders
  • 908gboneless skinless chicken breasts sliced 1" thick against the grain
  • 2 tspkosher salt
  • ½ tspgranulated sugar
  • 1 tspMSG
  • 1 tspcoarsely ground black pepper
  • 1knob fresh ginger (1"), peeled and grated
Chicken — Bone-In Wings
  • 908gwhole chicken wings drums and flats, not separated
  • 2 tspkosher salt
  • ½ tspgranulated sugar
  • 1 tspMSG
  • 1 tspcoarsely ground black pepper
  • 1knob fresh ginger (1"), peeled and grated
Dry Dredge
  • 300gpotato starch
  • 150gall-purpose flour
  • 2½ tspkosher salt
  • ½ tspgranulated sugar
  • 1 tspMSG
  • 1 tbspgarlic powder
  • 1 tspground white pepper
Wet Batter (reserve ⅓ cup / 50g from dry dredge above)
  • 50greserved dry dredge
  • 1½ tspbaking powder
  • 180mlcold water plus more as needed
Frying & Garnish
  • 2–3 qtcanola or peanut oil, for frying
  • —toasted sesame seeds, for garnish
  • 3green onions, thinly sliced for garnish
Method
1
Make the Sauce

Combine the gochujang, rice syrup, dark soy sauce, vinegar, sweet soy sauce, and ketchup in a small saucepan. Bring to a boil over medium heat, then simmer for 2–3 minutes, stirring occasionally. Off heat, stir in the minced garlic. Optionally steep 5 minutes then strain for a smoother glaze. Refrigerate until ready to use.

2
Marinate the Tenders

Slice chicken breasts against the grain into strips roughly 1" thick and 4–5" long. Toss in a large bowl with kosher salt, sugar, MSG, black pepper, and grated ginger until evenly coated. Cover and refrigerate for at least 1 hour — overnight is ideal.

2
Marinate the Wings

Toss the whole wings in a large bowl with kosher salt, sugar, MSG, black pepper, and grated ginger until evenly coated. Cover and refrigerate for at least 1 hour — overnight is ideal to let the flavors fully develop.

3
Prepare the Dredge & Batter

Whisk together the potato starch, flour, salt, sugar, MSG, garlic powder, and white pepper in a large bowl. Reserve ⅓ cup (50g) in a separate bowl; whisk in the baking powder, then gradually add cold water until you have a smooth, pourable batter. Drizzle a few tablespoons of wet batter back into the dry dredge and toss — this creates the extra-craggy, crispy bits.

4
Coat the Chicken

Pat the marinated chicken completely dry. Working one piece at a time, dip into the wet batter and let the excess drip off, then press firmly into the dry dredge on all sides until completely coated. Set on a baking sheet and repeat. Fry soon after coating.

5
First Fry — 325°F / 163°C

Heat oil in a heavy 6–7 qt pot to 325°F (163°C). Fry the tenders in batches — do not overcrowd — for 5–6 minutes, until lightly golden and just cooked through. Drain on a wire rack over a baking sheet. Repeat with remaining tenders.

5
First Fry — 325°F / 163°C

Heat oil in a heavy 6–7 qt pot to 325°F (163°C). Fry the wings in batches — do not overcrowd — for 8–10 minutes, until lightly golden but not deeply browned, and cooked through. Drain on a wire rack over a baking sheet. Repeat with remaining wings.

6
Second Fry — 375°F / 190°C

Raise the oil to 375°F (190°C). Skim any loose bits from the oil. Fry tenders again in batches for 1½–2 minutes, until deep golden and shatteringly crispy. Drain on the rack and let rest 1 minute.

6
Second Fry — 350°F / 177°C

Raise the oil to 350°F (177°C). Skim any loose bits from the oil. Fry wings again in batches for 2 minutes, until nicely golden brown and extra crispy. Drain on the rack and let the crust set for 1–2 minutes.

7
Sauce & Serve

Transfer the hot chicken to a large bowl, pour the gochujang glaze over top, and gently toss until every piece is evenly coated. Arrange on a platter, garnish with toasted sesame seeds and sliced green onions. Serve immediately.

Notes

Slicing tenders: Cut against the grain, about 1" thick and 4–5" long. Even thickness means even cooking throughout.

Hotter second fry: Tenders use 375°F (vs. 350°F for wings) — boneless pieces lose heat faster, so the extra temperature is needed to achieve the shatter.

Don't separate the wings: Keep drums and flats whole — they fry more evenly and look more impressive on the plate.

Cooler second fry: Wings use 350°F for the second fry. The bone retains heat well, so the lower temp avoids over-browning before the crust sets.

Double-frying is non-negotiable: The first fry cooks the chicken through; the second drives out moisture and delivers the crust. Never skip it.

Overnight marinade: One hour works fine, but overnight transforms the flavor and keeps the meat juicy through the high-heat fry.

Crispy bits trick: Drizzling wet batter into the dry dredge before coating creates extra craggy texture — don't skip this step.

Sauce timing: Toss in sauce right before serving. Hold unsauced chicken on the rack if needed and sauce to order.
