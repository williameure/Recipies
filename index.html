Paulie's-Style Rigatoni Bolognese

A faithful recreation of the iconic Houston classic

    Instructions
    1. 1
      Build the soffritto

      Heat olive oil and butter in a large Dutch oven over medium heat. Add onion, carrots, and celery. Cook, stirring often, until very soft and golden, about 10 minutes. Add garlic and cook 1 more minute.

    2. 2
      Brown the meat

      Add ground beef and veal. Break up as finely as possible — the smaller the crumble, the better it clings to pasta. Cook until no longer pink, 6–8 minutes. Season generously with salt and pepper.

    3. 3
      Add milk

      Pour in the whole milk and stir. Cook until fully absorbed, about 5 minutes. This tenderizes the meat and adds richness.

    4. 4
      Deglaze with wine

      Add red wine and scrape up any browned bits from the bottom. Cook until mostly absorbed, about 5 minutes.

    5. 5
      Simmer the sauce

      Stir in crushed tomatoes. Bring to a boil then reduce to low. Simmer partially covered for 1 hour, then uncovered for 30 more minutes. Sauce should be thick, not runny.

    6. 6
      Finish with cream

      Stir in heavy cream and Parmesan. Taste and adjust seasoning.

    7. 7
      Cook the pasta

      Cook rigatoni in well-salted boiling water until just al dente (1–2 min less than package). Reserve 1 cup pasta water before draining.

    8. 8
      Marry pasta and sauce

      Add drained rigatoni to the sauce over medium-low heat. Toss well, adding pasta water as needed so the sauce clings to every ridge. Cook together 1–2 minutes.

    9. 9
      Serve

      Plate and top with fresh basil, parsley, and extra Parmesan. Serve immediately.

    Notes & Tips
    The pasta secretLook for bronze-die-cut rigatoni ("trafilata al bronzo") — the rough surface grabs sauce like fresh pasta. Rustichella d'Abruzzo is a great brand.
    Make aheadThe bolognese is better the next day. Make the sauce ahead and reheat gently before cooking your pasta.
    Veal substituteCan't find ground veal? Use half beef, half ground pork for similar richness.
    Wine tipUse a dry red you'd drink — Chianti is the classic. A $12–15 bottle is perfect.
    Covered vs. uncoveredSimmer partially covered for the first hour, then uncovered for the last 30 minutes to thicken.
