Instructions
  1. 1
    Build the soffritto

    Heat olive oil and butter in a large Dutch oven over medium heat. Add onion, carrots, and celery. Cook, stirring often, until very soft and golden, about 10 minutes. Add garlic and cook 1 more minute.

  2. 2
    Brown the meat

    Add ground beef and veal. Break up as finely as possible — the smaller the crumble, the better it clings to pasta. Cook until no longer pink, about 6–8 minutes. Season generously with salt and pepper.

  3. 3
    Add milk

    Pour in the whole milk and stir. Cook until fully absorbed, about 5 minutes. This tenderizes the meat and adds richness.

  4. 4
    Deglaze with wine

    Add red wine and scrape up any browned bits. Cook until mostly absorbed, about 5 minutes.

  5. 5
    Simmer the sauce

    Stir in crushed tomatoes. Bring to a gentle boil, then reduce to low. Simmer partially covered for 1 hour, then uncovered for 30 more minutes. Sauce should be thick, not runny.

  6. 6
    Finish with cream

    Stir in heavy cream and Parmesan. Taste and adjust seasoning. Rich but light — not heavy or oily.

  7. 7
    Cook the pasta

    Cook rigatoni in well-salted boiling water until just al dente (1–2 min less than the package says). Reserve 1 cup of pasta water before draining.

  8. 8
    Marry pasta and sauce

    Add drained rigatoni directly to the sauce over medium-low heat. Toss well, adding splashes of pasta water to help the sauce cling to the ridges. Cook together 1–2 minutes.

  9. 9
    Serve

    Plate and top generously with fresh basil, parsley, and extra Parmesan. Serve immediately.

```
Notes & Tips
The pasta secretLook for bronze-die-cut dried rigatoni ("trafilata al bronzo") — the rough surface grabs the sauce like fresh pasta. Rustichella d'Abruzzo is a great brand.
Make aheadThe bolognese is better the next day. Make the sauce in advance and reheat gently before cooking your pasta.
Veal substituteIf you can't find ground veal, use half beef and half ground pork for similar richness.
Wine tipUse a dry red you'd drink — Chianti is the classic. A $12–15 bottle is perfect.
